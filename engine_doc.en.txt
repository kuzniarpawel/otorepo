This document describes the public and internal helper API of the engine and the adopted
conventions. Keep it updated as the engine evolves.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
 CONVENTIONS AND COORDINATE SYSTEMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Head frame:  x = right, y = up (skull), z = forward (nose).
World: "up" axis = +y; world gravity = (0,-1,0).
Sides (ear/side): "L" = left ear, "P" = right ear.
Canals (canal): "posterior", "horizontal", "anterior".

Quaternion: q = [w, x, y, z]; rotates HEAD vectors → WORLD
            (v_world = q ⊗ v_head ⊗ q*).

Nystagmus components (anatomical, patient frame), returned as {h, v, t}:
   h: + to the right / − to the left        (horizontal)
   v: + upward / − downward         (vertical)
   t: + upper poles to the right / − to the left  (torsional)

Ewald's laws used in the engine:
   I   — plane specificity: the fast-phase axis of a given canal (quickPhase)
   II  — horizontal canal: AMPULLOPETAL flow is excitatory; excitation > inhibition
   III — vertical canals: AMPULLOFUGAL flow is excitatory

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
 PUBLIC API (returned from the module)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

— STAGE 0 (rule-based) ——————————————————————————————————————————————

isExcitatory(canal, flow) -> bool
   Whether the given flow direction excites the canal (Ewald's law II/III).
   flow: "ampullopetal" | "ampullofugal".
   Returns: horizontal → (flow==="ampullopetal"); vertical → (flow==="ampullofugal").

quickPhase(canal, ear) -> {h, v, t}
   Fast-phase axis for canal EXCITATION, ipsiversive to the ear (Ewald I).
   ear: "L"|"P". (ipsi = ear==="P" ? +1 : -1)
   horizontal → {h:ipsi, v:0,    t:0}      (horizontal toward the excited ear; torsion masked)
   posterior  → {h:0,    v:+0.81,t:ipsi}   (upbeat + torsion toward the ear; real v:t≈0.81:1)
   anterior   → {h:0,    v:-1,   t:0}      (PURE downbeat; geometric torsion ~0.74 MASKED — NYS_SHOW.anterior.t=false)
   NOTE — CLINICAL MASK (NYS_SHOW): quickPhase returns the UI convention for BPPV (anterior = pure downbeat,
   horizontal without torsion). The FULL geometry (anterior torsion PRESERVED, for lateralization) → NeuroVOR.qpFull()
   / Vestibular.nysMag(); used by the HINTS layer (vHIT saccade / verticalBeat / SCDS). See the "CLINICAL MASK" section.

nysMag(canal, ear) -> {h, v, t}
   Full geometric proportions of the nystagmus from REAL canal normals (CANAL_NORMALS), normalized
   to max=1. Does NOT apply the NYS_SHOW clinical mask (anterior-canal torsion PRESERVED) — cf. quickPhase
   (same geometry, masked to the BPPV convention). Source of the full geometry for NeuroVOR
   (qpFull → vHIT saccade / verticalBeat / pressureStimulus-SCDS). ear: "L"|"P".

nystagmus({canal, ear, excited=true}) -> {h, v, t}
   Fast phase for the given, excited/inhibited canal.
   Inhibition (excited=false) reverses the sign of all components.
   Future use: unilateral loss (excited:false), push-pull.

— STAGE 1 (static geometry) ————————————————————————————————————

gHead(q) -> [x, y, z]
   The gravity vector expressed in the head frame for orientation q.
   Upright-standing head (q=[1,0,0,0]) → [0,-1,0].

(qSupineYaw / qPitch — REMOVED 2026-08-05. They encoded the old pose convention: a fixed −100°
   hang for every lying pose. Clinical pose orientations are NO longer engine API — they are built by
   stepHeadQ(body, yaw, face) over the POSE_SPEC table in src/pose/maneuvers.js:
   headQ = Rz(roll)·Rx(pitch)·Ry(yaw+dyaw); every angle has its source in a sentence of the instruction.)

position({canal, side, variant, q}) -> {excited, mag, h, v, t}
   Static stimulation of the AFFECTED canal in head orientation q.
   side: "L"|"P"; variant: "canalo" (geotropic) | "cupulo" (apogeotropic).
   Mechanism: projection of gravity onto the canal's excitation axis → flow → Ewald.
     drive = g · tangAt(G, φ_eval);  proj = G.exc · drive;  excited = proj>0 (ONE Ewald rule)
     φ_eval = restPhi(canal,side) for FREE debris (canalo; also cupulo of the VERTICAL canals — an
       explicit phenomenological choice, see the code comment) · CUPULA_DEG for HORIZONTAL-CANAL CUPULO.
       [2026-08-13, assessment II A1/V4: cupulolithiasis debris sits ON THE CUPULA, not at the free-debris
        rest (199.8°); the old proj<0 inversion held the Roll sign only by an antipodal coincidence
        (restPhi HC ~197° from the ampulla), while computing the null point on the WRONG side and
        reversing apo Bow&Lean against the Choung rule and the card own text. After the fix: apo Roll
        with an INTRINSIC asymmetry 0.832 (affected down) / 0.885 (healthy), null point at yaw +8..9°
        toward the AFFECTED ear (clinically 10–30°), apo B&L per the rule (bow→healthy, lean→affected —
        the arrow agrees with the label), pseudo-SN when sitting 0.052.]
     G.exc = +1 vertical canals (Ewald III: ampullofugal excites) · −1 horizontal (Ewald II).
     [2026-08-05] It used to be proj = g·GEXC[canal] (vertical) or g·uHC(side) (horizontal) — both of
     those axes are the tangent taken at φ=90°, i.e. at the very constant restPhi removed. The test
     card could therefore show a direction OPPOSITE to the dynamics of the same pose. GEXC and uHC
     have been DELETED.
   excited: bool; mag = |proj| (raw force); {h,v,t} = quickPhase with the sign of excitation.
   VALIDATED: Dix-Hallpike (posterior), Roll (geo/apo), APO Bow & Lean (consistent with the card since
   V4); CANALO Bow & Lean remains a watershed/positional-history matter (R8/R10 — plan V5).

— STAGE 2 (particle dynamics) ————————————————————————————————————————

simulateCanalith({canal, side, timeline, q0=null, phi0=null, settled=true, xi0=0, bond0=null, dt=0.05, tauP=6.5, tauC=5, gc=1.6, phiExit=null, fStat=0.04, adh=0.2, size="medium", rep=0, fatTau=2.0, fatFloor=0.06, crusArc=12, crusGrav=0.6}) -> [{t, xi, phi, exited}, ...] (+ a .final property)
   phiExit=null (default) = the MEASURED arc span per canal: ARC_SPAN {anterior 281.1 · posterior 318.8 ·
   horizontal 267.3}. (The crusFling/crusFlingRate parameters were REMOVED 2026-08-05 — see the crus chamber below.)
   VALIDATIONS (since 2026-08-13, assessment II A10): dt>0 · tauP>0 · tauC>dt/2 (Euler stability) ·
   gc>0 · fStat≥0 · adh≥0 · 0≤crusArc<phiExit−3 · crusGrav>0 — a clear RangeError instead of silent
   nonsense (gc<0 flipped the nystagmus side, crusArc>phiExit killed the test without a trace).
   since V3 also phi0 ∈ [CUPULA_DEG, phiExit] (described below); since V10 also bond0 ∈ [0,1] and finite xi0.
   Simulation of CANALITHIASIS: the otolith as a particle on the canal arc.
   [PHENOMENOLOGICAL MODEL — a proxy for the dynamics, not full hydrodynamics; see the "MODEL NATURE" header
    and "SIMPLIFICATIONS RELATIVE TO FULL MODELS".]
   timeline: array of segments [{q, tTrans, tHold, pivot}, ...]
       q      — target orientation (quaternion)
       tTrans — transition time to q [s] (slerp interpolation from the previous q)
       tHold  — hold time at q [s]
       pivot  — the step's rotation axis: "body" (hips/couch, default) | "neck" (the head alone);
                selects the lever arm of the R7 inertial term (LEVER.body 0.75 m / neck 0.12 m)
   phi0 — explicit starting arc angle of the debris [°]; null (default) = restPhi(canal,side): the
       gravitational minimum in the upright posture, or the cupula when that minimum lies outside the arc
       (posterior 48.8° · horizontal 199.8° · anterior 3°).
       VALIDATED since 2026-08-13 (assessment II, A9/V3): RangeError outside [CUPULA_DEG, phiExit] — no more
       silently dead simulations on NaN, debris resurrected from outside the canal (phi0>phiExit used to give
       a full liberatory transient) or teleports. A start in the CRUS ZONE (phi0 > phiExit−crusArc, vertical
       canals) = the debris STARTS IN THE CHAMBER (inCrus) instead of the old backward snap to pcrus.
   settled — the adhesion state at start (assessment II, A2/V3). true (default) = SETTLED debris: the
       adhesion gate from the resting drive; false = FRESHLY DISPLACED debris (positional history
       deposited it moments ago; the bond has not had time to form) — starts without the gate. The same
       distinction — "held by the bond vs held by gravity" — with which the engine explains the missing
       latency of the anterior canal. Without settled=false the gate quenched to 0.000 even responses
       of free amplitude |ξ|>1 (Bow & Lean: dead bands ~20% of the arc, the 205° cliff).
       THE GATE AT THE STARTING POINT (since V10, assessment II D1 — closure of artefact A2): for
       phi0≠null the resting drive is computed as driveAt(canal, side, phi0, q0-or-upright) — WHERE the
       debris lies, and in the STARTING orientation; for phi0=null LITERALLY the old restDrive(restPhi,
       upright) — the same bits, the default path untouched (proof V3: 34 scenarios × 48,144 samples,
       max difference 0; proof V10: snapshot dom=110 byte-identical PASS before extending the oracles).
       The old gate froze debris parked after a Dix (φ₀=159.5°, sitting: |driveAt|=0.142>fStat while
       |restDrive(restPhi)|≈0 → STUCK) for 2.05 s of bond consumption at someone else's point.
   xi0 — the cupular deflection at start (since V10; default 0). Carries the ξ tail of the previous run:
       chaining through {phi0, xi0, bond0, settled:true} is BIT-FOR-BIT identical to a single timeline
       (measured: max|Δ(ξ,φ)| = 0.0e+0 over the 1432 samples of the second act) — the B7 resolution
       stands on this identity (below, the FATIGABILITY section).
   bond0 — the bond state at start as a FRACTION of full adhesion ∈ [0,1] (since V10; null = 1). The
       unit is INDEPENDENT of size — the engine multiplies internally by the rescaled adh·r, which
       closes the documented footgun of chaining the raw final.bond between runs of different size.
       bond0=0 ≡ settled:false (the gate term (bond0==null || bond0>0) — continuity at zero).
   .final — the FINAL STATE {phi, xi, bond, stuck, exited, inCrus, bondFrac} as a property ON THE RESULT
       ARRAY (JSON.stringify of arrays skips it — oracles untouched). For session chaining (D1):
       pre=simulateCanalith(history); test=simulateCanalith({phi0: pre.final.phi, xi0: pre.final.xi,
       bond0: pre.final.bondFrac, settled:true, …}) — the chain is bit-for-bit identical to a single
       thread. bondFrac = the bond as a fraction [0,1] (clamped; the ONLY form for chaining); the raw
       bond (internal units adh·r) stays for compatibility — the between-size footgun is documented.
   q0 — STARTING orientation (quaternion); default null = the q of the FIRST segment (backward compatibility).
       NOTE (semantics of the 1st segment): with q0=null the first segment does NOT interpolate from the neutral position —
       its tTrans is time ALREADY IN the starting position, NOT a transition from sitting. To force a transition from a given
       orientation to the 1st q, pass q0 (e.g. [1,0,0,0] = neutral head). UI convention: the 1st segment has tTrans:0,
       and the transition is realized by the NEXT segment (maneuverTimeline: tTrans:0.8 per step). Analogously for simulateCupulolith.
   Model:
       φ — arc angle of the particle (ampulla φ=0, exit = ARC_SPAN[canal]: 267–319° per canal); lower
       bound CUPULA_DEG=3° (0.17 mm from the ampulla CENTRE — a point inside the ampulla) with a ZERO
       exit margin (the arc-end asymmetry noted in assessment II, C6 — sensitive to it: the horizontal
       emptying time with the healthy ear down and the rest-degeneracy threshold at a +9.9° pitch).
       Overdamped motion (Stokes drag, no inertia):
           dφ/dt = (g_head · tangent(φ)) / tauP
       Cupula (first-order model, long time constant):
           dξ/dt = -ξ/tauC + gc · exc · dφ/dt   (exc: +1 vertical / -1 horizontal)
   OTOLITH ADHESION (latency):
       The otolith is initially attached; the bond (adh) is broken by a sustained tangential force
       above the threshold fStat → a delayed start = LATENCY. A stronger provocation = shorter
       latency (consistent with the clinic). Once broken, the otolith no longer re-attaches in this session.
   COMMON-CRUS CHAMBER + EXPULSION INTO THE UTRICLE (VERTICAL canals: posterior+anterior; the horizontal has no crus):
       Anatomically, "reaching the end of the arc" (φ=phiExit) ≠ "falling into the utricle" — these are TWO events.
       • HORIZONTAL CANAL: no common crus → the non-ampullary end opens DIRECTLY into the utricle: after φ≥phiExit
         the otolith enters the utricle ONE-WAY (exited), at a PHYSICAL speed derived from gravity (~7–8 °/s).
       • VERTICAL CANALS (crusArc/crusGrav): after reaching the crus (φ≥phiExit−crusArc) the otolith ENTERS
         THE CRUS CHAMBER and PARKS — it leaves the sensitive canal, the cupula relaxes (ξ→0, no nystagmus), it waits for
         expulsion into the utricle. Condition: −f[1] > crusGrav, where f is the SPECIFIC FORCE f=g−a (R7); it is
         realized GRAVITATIONALLY — with the head upright (sitting) the utricle is below the crus → the debris
         falls in. Gives Epley expulsion at sitting up + a LIBERATORY nystagmus where it clinically occurs.

         in this model. The centripetal term a=ω×(ω×d) with the lever d=[±EAR_X, L>0, 0] gives a[1]≤0,

   CONSTANTS CALIBRATED TO THE LITERATURE (posterior canal, canalithiasis) — NUMBERS IN FORCE 2026-08-13

   Returns: t [s], xi (sign = excitation/inhibition), phi [°], exited (bool).
   Tuning parameters: tauP, tauC, gc, phiExit, fStat, adh.
   PARTICLE SIZE/DENSITY (size): "small" | "medium" (default) | "big" — or a number (radius multiplier r).
       Mapping SIZE_R = {small:0.78, medium:1.0, big:1.35}. Physical scaling from r:
         tauP ∝ r⁻²  (Stokes settling velocity v ∝ r² → faster flow for a larger particle),
         gc   ∝ r³   (displaced volume/mass of endolymph → cupular deflection),
         adh  ∝ r    (detachment force of a sphere from the wall, JKR/DMT scaling).
       medium == r=1 → result IDENTICAL to the previous calibration (safe default).
       Emergently: LARGE = shorter latency, stronger/faster nystagmus, faster exit;
       SMALL = long latency, a weak and slow course and a LONGER minimal hold (see the minimal-holds
       table in VALIDATION STATUS; the app's hold is DERIVED — R4b — so small still clears) —
       consistent with the rationale of ~30 s holds for slowly settling fragments (Hain [2]) and with
       "extremely long-latency BPPV" [11]. Addresses the simplification "no effect of particle size"
       from the SIMPLIFICATIONS section.
   FATIGABILITY (rep) — a DIFFERENTIATING criterion canalithiasis↔cupulolithiasis [12].
       rep = the provocation repetition number in the same session (0 = first = full response). Mechanism:
       a compact cluster of otoconia DISPERSES on repetition (serial Dix–Hallpike) into smaller, less
       coherent fragments → weaker aggregate cupular deflection. The factor fatigueFactor(rep,{fatTau,fatFloor})
       scales gc → the AMPLITUDE ξ (and the nystagmus) decreases linearly; GEOMETRY/LATENCY UNCHANGED (the dominant,
       undisputed clinical effect is the amplitude drop). Defaults fatTau=2.0, fatFloor=0.06; rep=0 → factor 1
       → IDENTICAL result (backward compatibility). Example (posterior canal): peak ξ 0.81→0.51→0.33→0.22→0.15 for
       rep 0..4. CUPULOLITHIASIS (simulateCupulolith) has NO rep — the debris adheres to the cupula, does not disperse
       → does NOT fatigue; the difference is an emergent differentiating feature.
       B7-RESOLUTION (2026-08-14, V10): a series in the same session = a STATE CHAIN (canonical) — a
       repeated provocation is a new run with {phi0, xi0, bond0} from the previous final (bit-for-bit
       identical to a single timeline), COMPOSED with rep (dispersion within the same call). Emergently:
       the second provocation comes WITHOUT mechanical latency (the bond is broken) and weaker
       (position × dispersion); artefact B9 (threshold latency growing with rep at constant mechanics)
       disappears, because a fresh-start-with-rep is no longer the model of a repetition within the
       session. rep ALONE (a fresh start) = dispersion/ensemble averaging — semantics from outside the
       session. Between visits the bond regrows (readhesion, the domain layer) and rep returns to 0
       (re-aggregation of the clump). ANCHORS: the fatigability mechanism is mainly POSITIONAL
       (Imai 2021/2025/2026, Boselli 2014, Yang & Gao 2022 — see R10-STATUS); dispersion = a secondary
       hypothesis, the card does not state it as fact.

fatigueFactor(rep=0, {fatTau=2.0, fatFloor=0.06}) -> factor ∈ [fatFloor, 1]
   Pure fatigability factor: fatFloor + (1−fatFloor)·exp(−rep/fatTau); rep 0/negative/NaN → 1; monotonic
   decay to fatFloor; fatTau≤0 → RangeError. Exported to UI/diagnostics (serial Dix–Hallpike).

nystagmusPhase(xi, thr=0.05) -> "primary" | "reversed" | "none"
   Classification of the phase relative to the provoking direction:
   "primary" (ξ>thr, primary/liberatory), "reversed" (ξ<-thr, reversed), "none".
   NOTE (assessment II, C3): a helper API, currently NOT WIRED UP in src (no module calls it);
   the 0.05 threshold is in a different unit than the UI visibility threshold (0.10 on strength AFTER rectification).

simulateCupulolith({canal, side, timeline, q0=null, dt=0.05, tauCup=0.8, gain=1.0, size="medium"}) -> [{t, xi, target}, ...]
   q0 — STARTING orientation; semantics as in simulateCanalith (null = the q of the first segment).
   VALIDATIONS (since 2026-08-13, assessment II A10/DYN-9): tauCup>dt/2 (below it the explicit Euler
   scheme DIVERGES — 0.024 gave ξ~8e26 with no error) · gain finite, DELIBERATELY without a sign
   constraint (gain<0 = future light cupula: persistent GEOTROPIC DCPN — assessment II, D3).
   size scales gain ∝ r³ (a heavier otoconia cluster → stronger cupular deflection); no latency
   (tauCup unchanged). SIZE_R as in simulateCanalith. medium == r=1 → identical result.
   Simulation of CUPULOLITHIASIS: otoliths attached to the cupula → a heavy cupula deflected
   DIRECTLY by gravity (static projection), without a free particle in the canal lumen.
   Model:
       target = CUP_WEAK · gain · |proj| · (excited ? +1 : -1)
                where proj/excited from position({canal,side,variant:"cupulo",q}) (Ewald's rule;
                horizontal: apogeotropic = reversed relative to geotropic)
                CUP_WEAK=0.45 — CUPULOLITHIASIS WEAKER THAN CANALITHIASIS [1] (debris attached to the cupula
                deflects it more weakly than a bolus of free otoconia driving the endolymph column). The SAME
                coefficient scales the diagnostic nystagmus of the "cupulo" variant (nysFromGeom) — a single source of truth.
                [RECALIBRATION 0.6→0.45, 2026-08-13 (assessment II B2/V4): calibrated to the OBSERVED
                 quantity — the emergent apo/geo ratio of ξ peaks was 0.736 at 0.6 (just OUTSIDE the
                 literature band 0.4–0.7); 0.45 gives 0.538 = mid-band. For the vertical canals the band
                 is an extrapolation.]
       dξ/dt = (target - ξ) / tauCup           (fast relaxation)
   Emergent features (contrast to canalithiasis):
       - NO LATENCY (ξ follows gravity immediately; tauCup small)
       - PERSISTENCE (ξ holds the target as long as the position is maintained; no extinction)
       - SMALLER AMPLITUDE (CUP_WEAK): nystagmus less intense than in canalithiasis [1]
       - smooth reversal on position change
   Returns: t [s], xi (sign = excitation/inhibition), target (static target).
   Tuning parameters: tauCup, gain — TO BE CALIBRATED.

   Conversion of instantaneous ξ (from simulateCanalith) into nystagmus components.
   excited = (xi>0); direction = quickPhase with the sign of excitation.
   intensity = min(1, |xi| · (excited ? 1 : 0.45))
               — Ewald II rectification: the inhibitory response is weaker.

ANNOTATION — LATENCY (a deliberate modeling choice):
  Our latency arises from otolith ADHESION (a bond broken by a sustained tangential
  force). In the literature this is an ACCEPTED but MINORITY hypothesis, invoked
  especially for long latencies (Hain/dizziness-and-balance; "Extremely Long
  Latency BPPV" 2017). The MAINSTREAM [1,2] explains latency by the ampulla→narrow-duct
  geometry (pressure amplification), which we do NOT implement. Other hypotheses:
  inertia of the otolith clot. We reproduce the correct clinical behavior
  (latency present, shorter with a stronger provocation) by an alternative route.

SIMPLIFICATIONS RELATIVE TO FULL MODELS:
  - a "tangential gravity → flow" proxy instead of the full transcupular pressure ΔPc;
  - no amplification in the narrow duct (the main latency mechanism per [1]);
  - particle size: SUPPORTED as a scaling parameter (size → tauP∝r⁻², gc∝r³, adh∝r);
    still no wall interactions and no velocity storage;
  - cupulolithiasis WEAKER than canalithiasis [1]: MODELED by a single coefficient CUP_WEAK=0.45
    (amplitude multiplier of ξ in simulateCupulolith AND of the diagnostic nystagmus of the "cupulo" variant in nysFromGeom);
    residual simplification: the coefficient is GLOBAL (not per-canal or per-geometry), a calibration value;
  - fatigability (dispersion of the compact debris on repetition) MODELED as a factor scaling
    amplitude (fatigueFactor / the rep parameter in simulateCanalith) — NOT as an explicit simulation of many
    particles with different trajectories; cupulolithiasis is deliberately without fatigability (a differentiating feature) [12].

GOAL AND PHILOSOPHY ("first-principles")
 We do not code the APPEARANCE of a pathology, but the PHYSIOLOGY: the resting activity of the labyrinths,
 Ewald's law with an inhibitory cut-off, fixation suppression (flocculus), canal VOR gain,
 the neural integrator, and the graviceptive axis (otoliths/MLF). The signs — spontaneous
 nystagmus, its dependence on fixation and gaze, the head impulse (vHIT) result,
 skew deviation — FOLLOW from the asymmetry of a few parameters. A layer INDEPENDENT of
 the BPPV dynamics (simulateCanalith); it shares only the direction conventions (quickPhase)
 and the frontal camera (mirror cam.right[0] = -1, like nysFromGeom/nysFromDyn).
 It realizes the todo item: "Other states (excited:false / push-pull): unilateral
 vestibulopathy, central nystagmus".

CONVENTIONS
 Sides: "P"=right=+x (right ear), "L"=left=-x. Afferent frequencies in Hz.
 Slow-phase velocities (SPV) and drift in °/s. Gaze angle gazeDeg: + = to the right (P).
 The "head-frame" sign h/t as in Vestibular; `dir`/`tdir` = the SCREEN sign after camera mirroring.

PUBLIC API (returned from the module)

 afferent(tone, drive) -> Hz                                                                  [H1][H2]
   The afferent after RECTIFICATION: max(0, min(R_SAT, tone+drive)). Inhibitory cut-off (≥0) +
   saturation (≤R_SAT=400; until stage N3 of review II it was 300 — a healthy VOR was losing gain from as low as 262°/s). A shared primitive: rest (drive=0) and head impulse (drive≠0).

 spontaneous(p) -> {kind,h,v,t,dir,tdir,vdir,strength,spv,beatEar,lesionEar}                  [H1][H2]
   STAGE 1 — SPONTANEOUS nystagmus from tonic asymmetry (push-pull). The brain estimates head velocity
   from the difference of the horizontal-pair afferents: Ω_est ∝ (rateL − rateR). A tonic asymmetry mimics a steady
   rotation → the SLOW phase toward the weaker labyrinth, the FAST phase (the visible beat) toward the stronger one.
   spv = SPV_MAX·|toneL−toneR|/R0 (SPV_MAX=30°/s, R0=90). Horizontal-torsional nystagmus (tH = 0.5·h·asym).
   Direction FIXED regardless of gaze — a PERIPHERAL feature.
   STAGE 7 (E3): a VERTICAL-TORSIONAL component from the vertical-canal pairs (verticalBeat) is added to the horizontal —
   fields v/vdir, tV, t=tH+tV, spvV/strengthV; kind="verticalTorsional" when the vertical dominates. Vertical canals
   healthy → v=0, spvV=0 (the horizontal case unchanged). See verticalBeat (STAGE 7).
   TWO SCALE FIXES from the neurophysiology review:
     • SPV_MAX 12→30°/s. An acute UVH in darkness beats at ~20–40°/s (neuritis, the first days), so the old
       model CEILING (12°/s with a completely silent labyrinth) lay BELOW the real range. The effect was
       visible on screen: with fixationGain 0.9 an acute neuritis gave 1.1°/s in the light, i.e. less
       than the module's own VIS_THRESH=2 threshold — the pathology came out "invisible" at the bedside. Now 2.8°/s.
     • tH weighted by intensity (·asym). The VERTICAL components carry intensity (the pair imbalance /R0),
       while the horizontal one carried a unit sign — so the sum t=tH+tV mixed direction with magnitude.
       The effect: a trace horizontal nystagmus (asym≈0.01) contributed the FULL 0.5 and could REVERSE the
       resultant torsion direction (tdir, i.e. what the renderer draws) of a strong vertical-torsional nystagmus.
   `kind` is decided by comparing the VISIBLE velocities (spvV vs spv), not |v| vs |h| — h is always
   ±1, so 0.1°/s of horizontal used to "win" over 12°/s of vertical.
   NOTE ON THE NAME `lesionEar`: it is the ear with the LOWER nucleus activity — it coincides with the affected
   ear only in a loss. In an IRRITATIVE lesion (irritative Ménière) the affected ear is MORE ACTIVE, so the
   actually affected side is carried by `trueLesionEar` (from compensate ← p.lesionEar, set e.g. by meniere()).

 suppressionFactor(p, fixOn) -> [0..~1.5]                                                       [H3]
 observe(p, fixOn) -> spontaneous(p) with suppression applied {spv,spvRaw,suppressionFactor,suppressed,...}
   STAGE 2 — FIXATION SUPPRESSION. eye = VOR − fixationGain·retinal_slip. fixOn=true (light):
   peripheral (fixationGain≈0.9) fades (×0.1); central (≈0) persists; <0 = paradoxical enhancement.
   fixOn=false (Frenzel/darkness): the full slow phase. `suppressed` = clinically "suppresses with fixation".

 headImpulse(p, toSide, {headVel=200, headAmp=15}) -> {gain,deficit,saccadePresent,saccadeAmp,
                                                        saccadeToSide,inhibitedFloored,abnormal,...}  [H4][H5]
   STAGE 3 — head impulse (vHIT). At the impulse velocity the INHIBITED canal hits the inhibitory cut-off
   (0 Hz, `inhibitedFloored`) → the VOR velocity is carried SOLELY by the EXCITED canal
   (ipsilateral). gain = the excited modulation / the ideal one. deficit → a corrective SACCADE (overt when
   ≥2.5° ≈ gain<0.83), directed opposite to the impulse (`saccadeToSide`). CENTRAL: gains≈1 →
   a normal HIT (ominous). Calibration S_HZ=0.8 → cut-off ~tone/S≈112°/s < the vHIT velocity.
   The afferent's DYNAMIC RANGE (a review fix): the excitatory modulation is bounded by a PROPERTY of the
   afferent, MOD_MAX=R_SAT−R0=310 Hz (after N3; helper excited(), shared with fusionWeights), and is not computed as
   rate−tone. Previously a high resting tone by itself ate up the headroom to the ceiling: a tone ≥167 Hz with gain=1.0
   gave a PATHOLOGICAL vHIT with no gain loss whatsoever — and a tone up to 200 Hz is reachable with the slider in
   the Laboratory. That contradicted the module's own thesis: in the IRRITATIVE phase of Ménière's the gain is
   preserved, so the vHIT stays NORMAL, and that is the DIFFERENTIATING feature of this entity [H20]. Saturation still
   acts where it makes physiological sense — at a very high head velocity (Ω>387°/s for gain 1.0 — outside the vHIT protocol; after N3, it was 262).
   STAGE 7 (E2): the second argument is POLYMORPHIC — 'P'/'L' (horizontal, as above, numerically unchanged) OR {canal,ear}
   (any of the 6 canals; planes HC/RALP/LARP). It also returns canal/ear/plane/coplanar/saccade{h,v,t}. See vhitPlane.

 gazeEvoked(p, gazeDeg) -> {tau,spv,beatSign,driftVel,present}                                  [H6]
 nystagmusAtGaze(p, gazeDeg, fixOn) -> the resultant nystagmus {beatHead,spv,dir,strength,components}
 directionChanging(p, fixOn, ecc=20) -> bool
   STAGE 4a — a "leaky" integrator: at gaze θ the drift toward center = θ/τ (slow phase), the fast phase
   toward the gaze. Healthy τ≈25 (negligible drift); central τ≈1–3 → a DIRECTION-CHANGING nystagmus.
   nystagmusAtGaze sums the beat velocities: SPONTANEOUS (peripheral, fixed) + GAZE-EVOKED (central, changing).
   directionChanging requires visibility ≥VIS_THRESH(2°/s) on BOTH sides and opposite directions
   (a strong spontaneous nystagmus can dominate the drift → it stays unidirectional — realistic).

 skew(p) -> {present,central,skewDeg,sign,hyperSide,torsionDeg}                                  [H7]
   STAGE 4b — skew deviation / OTR from graviceptive asymmetry (otoliths + MLF). present when |skewTone|≥1°.
   central when |skewTone|≥SKEW_CENTRAL(2.5°) — only a SIGNIFICANT skew counts as a CENTRAL feature (INFARCT);
   a small skew (e.g. the utricle of the superior nerve) present, but NOT central (avoids a false "stroke" — a review fix).
   Alternate-cover test: the uncovered eye catches fixation with a VERTICAL SACCADE = positive cover test = central.
 svv(p) -> {tiltSide, deg, abnormal, peripheralDeg, centralDeg, central}                          [H7][H22]
   STAGE 7 — SUBJECTIVE VISUAL VERTICAL (SVV). The graviceptive tilt computed from TWO tracks of the same axis:
     • PERIPHERAL — the utricle + the VERTICAL canals AC/PC; the vertical tilts TOWARD THE AFFECTED SIDE;
     • CENTRAL — skewTone (the otolith-brainstem/MLF pathway, the same one that gives skew and torsion), SVV_SKEW=1.6°/1°.
   `central` says WHICH track dominates — on that basis clinicalReadout attributes the finding as peripheral or
   as central, instead of (as before) always as peripheral. A review fix: svv() read the peripheral track
   only, so a patient with an overt OTR (skew 3°, torsion 4°) had SVV=0.0° = "normal" — whereas a skew without
   an SVV tilt clinically practically does not occur (SVV is the MOST SENSITIVE component of the OTR triad).
   MORE SENSITIVE than skew deviation: an INFERIOR neuritis gives a tilt DESPITE a normal skew (skew from the utricle=0).
   Symmetry (BVH)→0. abnormal when ≥2°. Does NOT affect the HINTS verdict (a supplementary readout).
 vemp(p) -> {cVEMP:{L,R,ampL,ampR,asym,weakEar,strongEar,bilateralWeak,organ,nerve}, oVEMP:{...}}  [H18][H22]
   STAGE 7 — VEMP: cVEMP(cervical)≈SACCULE→INFERIOR nerve (ipsi); oVEMP(ocular)≈UTRICLE→SUPERIOR nerve. Amplitude from the
   organ's function (sacculeL/R, utricleL/R) × the THIRD WINDOW enhancement (dehiscence ⇒ ×VEMP_SCDS=2.0 ipsilaterally).
   Status: ≥1.35 enhanced / ≥0.65 normal / ≥0.3 reduced / <0.3 absent. It separates the branches: INFERIOR
   neuritis→cVEMP↓+oVEMP normal; SUPERIOR→the opposite. Two review fixes:
     • bilateralWeak — a BILATERAL absence (BVH/ototoxicity) gives AR%≈0, so the mere lack of asymmetry read
       as "normal"; the same masking pitfall that the caloric handles with the bilateralWeak flag;
     • strongEar / the "enhanced" status — SCDS clinically INCREASES the amplitude (a large oVEMP n10, a low cVEMP
       threshold), and the old amplitude ceiling of 1.0 made that structurally impossible: a modelled dehiscence showed
       a "normal VEMP", i.e. the inverse of the diagnostic SCDS pair. weakEar names an ear only when its
       amplitude is ACTUALLY < 0.65 (otherwise an enhancement on one side would "reduce" the healthy other one).

 SCENARIOS, scenario(key) -> patient                                                            [H8]
   Presets: normal · neuritisR/neuritisL (PERIPHERAL: tone↓ + gain↓ of the affected canal, flocculus intact,
   integrator normal, no skew) · strokeCentral (CENTRAL: canals INTACT gain≈1 → a normal HIT,
   mild asymmetric tone, a "leaky" integrator, skew+torsion; skewTone=−3 = an ipsiversive OTR for a lesion
   on the P side, i.e. the medullary/Wallenberg variant — the commonest vascular cause of AVS). KEY: the STATIC tone (nystagmus) and the
   DYNAMIC gain (HIT) are SEPARATE parameters — that is why a stroke gives a nystagmus with a normal HIT.

 hints(p) -> {verdict, hi, vhitVertical, ny, ts, infarct, centralSigns}                          [H8][H22]
   STAGE 5 — HINTS synthesis. verdict: "normal" | "peripheral" (reassuring) | "central" (ominous).
   Central features (INFARCT): Impulse Normal (a normal HIT DESPITE a spontaneous nystagmus) ·
   Fast-phase Alternating (a direction-changing nystagmus) · Refixation on Cover Test (a positive skew) ·
   isolatedVertical (an isolated vertical-torsional nystagmus with a NORMAL vHIT of the vertical canals).
   The presence of any → "central". The reassuring triad (pathological HIT + unidirectional, fixation-suppressed
   + no skew) → "peripheral".
   TWO NYSTAGMUS AXES (a review fix). ny.horizontal / ny.vertical separate the horizontal component
   from the vertical-torsional one; ny.hasSpontaneous = the sum. Previously ONLY the horizontal counted, so a patient
   with a 9–12°/s vertical-torsional nystagmus (INFERIOR-nerve neuritis [H22]) got the verdict "normal" —
   and that ALONGSIDE the centralSigns list from clinicalReadout, which reported the same nystagmus as a red flag.
   The verdict and the sign list contradicted each other on one screen.
   The criteria are PER AXIS, because the HIT tests the HORIZONTAL canals:
     • Impulse Normal ← a HORIZONTAL nystagmus with a normal horizontal HIT;
     • isolatedVertical ← a VERTICAL nystagmus with a normal RALP/LARP vHIT (vhitVertical) — the exact

 compensate(p) -> {vnL, vnR, c, lesionEar, clampAmt, paceAmt, pacemakerBias}                    [H10][H11]
   STAGE 6 — STATIC compensation. The activity of the vestibular NUCLEI (VN) after neuroplasticity — it is this
   (not the raw labyrinth) that push-pull "sees" (spontaneous). Two-track: a CEREBELLAR CLAMP lowers the healthy
   nucleus (early, contribution exp(−c/CLAMP_TAU)) + an intrinsic PACEMAKER raises the affected nucleus (late, persistent).
   clamp+pacemaker = c·gap (restored) → symmetry at c=1 WITHOUT overshoot. c=0 and bias=0 → an exact
   NO-OP (vnL/vnR = afferent(tone,0)) — consistent with stages 1–5.
   STAGE 6b — pacemakerBias (sticky, [Hz]): a latched pacemaker charge INDEPENDENT of the current tone.
   When the labyrinth recovers function, the affected nucleus (tone returned + charged bias) becomes STRONGER →
   a RECOVERY nystagmus (Bechterew's) beating toward the EARLIER affected ear. The scalar c carries no history —
   which is why the charge is a separate, persistent parameter (flag spontaneous().bechterew).

 fusionWeights(p, headVel, toSide) -> {wCanal, wNeck, canalRel, neckRel}                        [H12][H13][H14]
   STAGE 6c — RELIABILITY-WEIGHTED FUSION (precision-weighted / complementary filter; an optimal estimator).
   The canal carries a velocity estimate with a weight = reliability (gain × the margin to saturation). At high velocity
   the canal loses reliability (saturation/inhibitory cut-off) → the estimator SWITCHES to neck
   proprioception/efference: neckRel = c·(1−canalRel). The "switch" FOLLOWS from the weights, not from a script. fw describes
   the MECHANISM (when/why the system switches to prediction).

 postRotational(p) -> {tauBase, tau, c, shortened}                                              [H10][H14]
   STAGE 6c — VELOCITY STORAGE. tau = tauVS·(1 − DVS_FRAC·c): compensation "opens the valve" of the velocity
   integrator → decay after rotation 15 s → ~5 s. A SEPARATE system from the gaze-holding integrator
   (integratorTau, stage 4a): compensation shortens velocity storage, but NOT gaze-holding (otherwise a false central).

 headImpulse — EXTENDED with DYNAMIC compensation: {covertAmp, overtAmp, covertFrac, covertSaccade,
   overtSaccade, fusion}. The total saccade = deficit·amp (the gain is IRREPARABLE), but it shifts from OVERT
   (overt, after the impulse, slip) to COVERT (covert, during the movement, prediction from fusion); the covert share = c
   (completeness of compensation): c=1 → the saccade is entirely COVERT (a bedside HIT "normal"). fusionWeights = the mechanism.
   abnormal = an INSTRUMENTAL deficit (independent of compensation); saccadePresent/overtSaccade = bedside
   overtness (vanishes with compensation). [H5][H12][H13]

SIMPLIFICATIONS / CAVEATS:
  - HORIZONTAL UVH nystagmus: torsion as a fixed fraction of the horizontal (TORS_FRAC=0.5) — illustrative; the VERTICAL canals
    (stage 7/E3) are composed realistically PER coplanar PAIR (verticalBeat), but with a simplified TORS_V=0.8;
    the HINTS RENDERER draws horizontal + VERTICAL + torsion (added in U4: neuroNysParams Av/vDir; the inferior-nerve downbeat visible);
    the animation is GATED by the visibility threshold (strength < VIS_THRESH/SPV_MAX → 0), so as not to show sub-threshold
    gaze-evoked drift of the healthy (τ=25 → 0.8°/s) — consistent with the label (spv<VIS_THRESH = "no nystagmus");
  - [EXTENDED in N7] vHIT: a BINAURAL gain (W_IN=1/3), velocity-dependent, with a pursuit term —
    still one number per impulse (without the full eyeball velocity dynamics and saccade metrics beyond amplitude);
  - a linear integrator (drift θ/τ) without horizontal/vertical asymmetry and without rebound nystagmus;
  - [RESOLVED in N7 of review II] Alexander's law: a multiplicative term ALEX_GAIN=0.02 (toward:away 2.5:1)
    + degrees I–III (alexanderGrade); it remains a LINEAR simplification without an adaptive term (rebound);
  - skew as a scalar vertical divergence; no full OTR geometry (head tilt, cyclotorsion) —
    we model the graviceptive axis as a SINGLE number (skewTone) shared by skew and SVV, without separating
    the level of the lesion (medulla=ipsiversive vs midbrain/INC=contraversive); the stroke preset assumes the
    MEDULLARY variant (Wallenberg) as the commonest vascular cause of AVS;
  - [RESOLVED in N3 of review II] fixationGain 0.65 by default → a fixation index of 0.35 (the middle of the
    clinical band 0.3–0.6); the old claim that a correction "would shift every readout" was REFUTED by probe
    (the suppressed≤0.5 and failsSuppression>0.5 thresholds have a buffer on both sides — zero flag changes);
  - pacemakerBias (Bechterew) requires an EXPLICIT lesionEar once both labyrinths have returned to symmetry: after
    the tones have equalized the affected side CANNOT be recovered from the parameters alone (the information is gone), so a
    charge without a stated side stays idle — a limitation of the model, not an error of the derivation;
  - compensation models a loss (HYPOfunction) only: gap=max(0,·). An IRRITATIVE asymmetry (the tone of the affected
    side > the healthy one) is not compensated — consistent with the clinic (a Ménière attack lasts minutes–hours,
    orders of magnitude too short for static compensation), but that is an assumption, not a result;
  - compensation: a single scalar c (without a time axis hours↔weeks); clamp/pacemaker as pure functions of c;
    the pacemaker charge (Bechterew) as a sticky parameter (a carrier of history), because the scalar c carries no memory;
  - neck-ocular fusion and velocity storage: phenomenological (weight ∝ reliability / c), without a full Kalman
    filter; HSN ADDED in N4 (a MONOphasic model — no biphasic reversal; a vertical "perverted HSN" after
    horizontal shaking DELIBERATELY not modelled: a simple flag from verticalBeat would fire falsely for a
    peripheral superior neuritis, and a correct model requires cross-coupled velocity storage);
  - HINTS as an EDUCATIONAL model — it does NOT replace an examination; valid in AVS with a persistent nystagmus,
    interpret with HINTS "plus" (hearing) and context (Tarnutzer 2011 [H9]). NOTE: a compensated peripheral loss
    can have a bedside HIT "normal" (covert saccades) — a pitfall when the AVS nystagmus is no longer present.
